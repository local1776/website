State Active Duty

Pay, Benefits and Injuries

Pay and Benefits

Guard members have long worried about State Active Duty (SAD) pay compared with Title 32 federal active duty pay or their technician pay, because SAD rates were traditionally much lower. Every state and territory we represent has since changed its laws, and SAD pay and allowances now match regular active duty.

Injuries

If you are injured during emergency operations, your status decides who pays. Working as a federal technician, you are covered by the federal workers’ compensation program, just as on a regular workday. On SAD, injury compensation is decided by your state.

22 Days of Military Leave

Law Enforcement Leave

Federal employees in the National Guard normally get 20 days (160 hours) of military leave each fiscal year for military duty. (Congress raised it from 15 days in the defense act signed December 23, 2024.) That goes fast, and if you are activated for a state mission with no comp time, military leave or annual leave left, your only option may be leave without pay from your civilian job.

But federal law provides an additional 22 workdays (176 hours) of military leave each calendar year for emergency duty, and for full-time service in support of a contingency operation. It is commonly called Law Enforcement Leave (LEL). It covers federal employees who perform military duty in support of civil authorities to protect life and property, or who serve full time on orders in support of a contingency operation.

The main difference from regular military leave, annual leave or comp time is that LEL does not let you collect both paychecks (see the third question below). But it does keep your civilian pay and benefits whole during the activation: you keep earning annual and sick leave, and your health insurance premiums, TSP contributions and loans, and other deductions keep getting paid, so you don’t come home to a debt.

LEL is charged by the hour, only for the hours you would have been at work, so the 176 hours normally cover about one continuous month of activation. That is a big benefit to weigh when you choose your leave status for a response.

Questions and Answers

When are employees eligible for the extra 22 days of military leave?
In two situations: (a) when performing military duty in support of civil authorities to protect life and property; or (b) when performing full-time military service on a call or order to active duty in support of a contingency operation.
An employee was activated by the governor for an emergency. Do they get the 22 days?
Yes. Guard members ordered to state emergency duty, in either a state or a federal status, are assisting civil authorities in protecting life and property. On top of regular military leave (unless on SAD), annual leave or comp time, they may use the extra 22 days to cover the absence.
Do employees get both military and civilian pay while on Law Enforcement Leave?
No. An employee receives the greater of their civilian or military pay, not both. Our fact sheet explains how.
