Background

National Guard Technicians Act of 1968

Dual status technicians account for the largest share of the National Guard’s full-time support force. This category of federal employee was created in 1968 by the National Guard Technicians Act. Technicians are unique because they must keep concurrent military membership in the Guard as a condition of their civilian job, and they are administered by their state or territory’s adjutant general, a state employee.

The Act has caused confusion since the start. The courts called technicians “hybrid employees,” and they were denied many of the rights and benefits their civil service counterparts enjoy, because of the way the Act grants administrative authority to the adjutants general. The Guard took full advantage of that technicality to avoid following federal law and regulation.

The Supreme Court Has Spoken

Ohio Adjutant General v. FLRA (2023)

The last attempt by a Guard state to strip technicians of their rights came in 2016, when the Ohio Adjutant General tried to dissolve the union there by cancelling payroll deductions and refusing to recognize the collective bargaining agreement. The union filed an unfair labor practice charge. The Ohio National Guard claimed the Federal Labor Relations Authority had no authority over it, because the Guard is not a federal agency and the adjutant general is a state employee. Guard states had made the same argument since 1968, and lost every time at the circuit courts, and Ohio had a 45-year bargaining relationship with AFGE Local 3970. The FLRA quickly ruled for the union, and Ohio challenged the decision.

In 2022 the 6th Circuit ruled for the FLRA, and Ohio appealed to the Supreme Court. LIUNA and the AFL-CIO filed a friend-of-the-court brief in support of the FLRA. Even with no conflict in the lower courts, the Court took the case and ruled, almost unanimously, that technicians are federal employees for the purposes of employment law, settling the matter for good. The Court found that the Civil Service Reform Act of 1978 lets the FLRA regulate the labor practices of state militias. The Merit Systems Protection Board has since applied the ruling to technician appeals, in Local 1776’s own case, Erdel v. Department of the Army.

Where We Stand Today

Restore Bargaining and Stop the AGR Conversion

In 2025, one executive order pulled most of the federal workforce out of the collective bargaining law. Our first priority is to bring collective bargaining back, written into law so the next executive order cannot take it away.

Our second is to stop the conversion of technician positions to Active Guard and Reserve (AGR) positions. Technicians cost taxpayers less, and the switch costs the employees who make it far more than the recruiting pitch admits.

TRICARE Reserve Select

Immediate TRICARE Reserve Select

When TRICARE Reserve Select was enacted in the late 2000s, the law barred federal employees eligible for the Federal Employees Health Benefits program from enrolling. Because technicians are federal employees eligible for FEHB, they were shut out of one of the key benefits available to every other Guard member.

LIUNA has lobbied since then to lift the ban. For more than a decade the obstacle was cost: Congress wanted to lift it for every federal employee who is also a reservist, not just technicians, and some studies put the price above $1 trillion. Congress finally lifted the ban in the late 2010s, but delayed it until January 1, 2030, to avoid the projected increase in mandatory defense spending.

Our goal is to bring that date closer to today.

Decades of Experience on Capitol Hill

Leo Gannon

Mr. Gannon has advised Local 1776 on legislative matters for over 10 years. His experience, expertise and personal relationships with Members of Congress and our partners in the labor movement are invaluable as we protect federal employee rights and push for improvements to the National Guard civilian employee program.

Before retiring, Mr. Gannon worked directly with Local 1776 on legislative matters as Assistant Director and Director of Legislative, Political and Governmental Affairs for LIUNA in Washington, D.C., a career with LIUNA spanning nearly 25 years. His work with Local 1776 was key to securing MSPB appeal rights, EEO protections and the Title 5 conversion of National Guard technicians between 2015 and 2018 — the first major changes to the National Guard Technicians Act since 1968.

A Lifetime of Public Policy Experience

Mr. Gannon has worked in public policy his entire career. Beyond federal employee and National Guard issues, he worked on transportation and infrastructure reauthorization, the Water Resources Development Act, comprehensive immigration reform and the Affordable Care Act. He has been involved in every facet of surface transportation, aviation and railroad funding, including public-private partnerships, as well as water and wastewater financing, Superfund and brownfields, EPA regulations, infrastructure financing and related construction and regulatory issues.

He has testified before Congress and several state legislatures, and in Ontario, Canada. He served on the executive board of the High-Speed Ground Transportation Association and toured Europe’s high-speed rail systems, including Germany’s Maglev.

His executive positions include President and CEO of the Gannon Consultancy Group, an executive at a national construction management firm, and Director of Business Development and Marketing for international architecture and engineering firms. He also served as executive director of a city planning department, a redevelopment agency, and housing and urban development programs.

Mr. Gannon served four terms as a Rhode Island State Senator and chaired the Senate Business Committee. He served on the Rhode Island Health Services Council and the Coastal Resources Committee, on the executive board of the National Conference of State Legislatures, and as a delegate to the Democratic National Convention Rules Committee, and he taught graduate courses in political communication at Emerson College in Boston. He is a committee member of the Tampa Bay chapter of the Council on Foreign Relations. A graduate of the University of Rhode Island and Harvard University, he and his wife Elizabeth live in Florida.
